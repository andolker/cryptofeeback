How to calculate trading fees on leveraged crypto futures

Most traders think fees are tiny. On leverage, they are not. Here is the exact math, so you know what every trade really costs.

5 min read · Updated November 2026

Fees are charged on the position, not your margin

This is the part most people miss. When you open a futures trade with leverage, the exchange charges its fee on the full position size, not on the money you put in.

Position size = margin × leverage
Fee = position size × fee rate
Round trip = open fee + close fee

A real example

You trade $10,000 of margin at 10x. That opens a $100,000 position. With a taker fee of 0.05%:

  • Opening the trade costs $50
  • Closing it costs another $50
  • One round trip: $100, or 1% of your margin

Now do it 3 times a day for 20 trading days. That is 60 trades and $6,000 a month in fees, whether those trades won or lost.

How leverage multiplies the bill

Same $10,000 margin, taker fee of 0.05% on both sides:

LeveragePositionFee per round trip60 trades / month
1x$10,000$10$600
5x$50,000$50$3,000
10x$100,000$100$6,000
20x$200,000$200$12,000
50x$500,000$500$30,000

At 50x, a single round trip costs 5% of your margin. The price has to move in your favor just to break even.

What is not included

Funding fees on perpetual contracts are separate. They are paid between traders every few hours and can be positive or negative. This guide covers trading fees only, the ones the exchange keeps.

At least 20% of those fees can come back to you every week. On $6,000 a month, that is $1,200.
