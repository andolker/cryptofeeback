What is trading fee cashback, and is it safe?

Getting money back on fees you already pay sounds too good to be true. It isn't. Here is where the money comes from and what you never have to give up.

4 min read · Updated November 2026

Where the money comes from

Exchanges pay partners a share of the trading fees from the users they bring in. It is a standard affiliate program, the same way exchanges pay influencers and communities.

The difference is what happens next. Most partners keep 100% of that share. A cashback service sends part of it back to you, the trader who actually paid the fee.

You pay the normal exchange fee
Exchange pays a share to its partner
You get back at least 20% of your fees

You don't pay more

Your fee rate stays exactly the same as any other user's. Cashback is not a discount you pay for. It is money that was already going to a partner, now coming back to you.

Is it safe?

A real cashback service never needs access to your money. Check for these three things:

  • No API keys. You should never have to connect your account or give trading permissions.
  • No deposits. Your funds stay in your own exchange account the whole time.
  • No passwords or seed phrases. Anyone who asks for them is not offering cashback.

If a "cashback" offer asks you to send funds, connect an API with trading rights or share a login, walk away.

How it works in 3 steps

  1. Open an account at a partner exchange through the cashback link.
  2. Trade as usual. Nothing changes in how you trade or what you pay.
  3. Get paid every week. Your share of the fees goes straight to your exchange account.

How much can it be?

It depends on your volume. A trader paying $6,000 a month in fees gets at least $1,200 back every month. Someone paying $300 a month gets at least $60. The more you trade, the more it adds up.
