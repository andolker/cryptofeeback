Maker vs taker fees: how to pay less on every trade

Two orders, same size, same price. One can cost less than half of the other. The difference is whether you add liquidity or take it.

4 min read · Updated November 2026

What is a taker?

You are a taker when your order fills immediately against orders already on the book. Market orders are always taker. A limit order priced to fill right away is taker too.

What is a maker?

You are a maker when your limit order sits on the order book and waits for someone else to fill it. You add liquidity, so exchanges charge you less.

The difference in dollars

Example on a $100,000 position with typical regular-user futures rates (0.02% maker, 0.05% taker):

How you enter and exitRound trip fee60 trades / month
Taker in, taker out$100$6,000
Maker in, taker out$70$4,200
Maker in, maker out$40$2,400

Five ways to pay less

  1. Use limit orders for entries. If you are not in a hurry, let price come to you.
  2. Turn on "post-only". It cancels the order instead of filling it as taker, so you never pay taker by accident.
  3. Take profit with limit orders. Set your target in advance as a resting order.
  4. Trade less, size better. Fewer, better trades cut fees faster than any discount.
  5. Get cashback on top. Whatever rate you pay, maker or taker, cashback returns part of it.

Do not trade your stop-loss for a lower fee. Stops usually fill as taker, and that is fine. Paying 0.05% is better than missing your exit.
